Start with a mission, not an app.

Before typing into an AI tool, decide what result you want. Today we're learning to reclaim time from one repeatable, low-risk business task.

What is AI good at? Making first drafts, reformatting existing information, brainstorming alternatives and organizing text. What is it not? A substitute for approval, expertise, privacy policy or fact checking.
Use fictional data in this preview. For real business work, follow your organization's policy and use an approved AI tool. Never paste passwords, payment details, confidential customer records or protected information into unapproved systems.

Would you send this to a customer?

A strong first draft is useful—but still needs a human review. Read the fictional customer note and the intentionally flawed AI response.

ORIGINAL CUSTOMER NOTE
"Hi, is the estimate ready yet? When should I expect it? Thanks, Sam."

WHAT THE BUSINESS KNOWS
The team is still reviewing details. No completion date or price has been confirmed.
ILLUSTRATIVE AI DRAFT — CONTAINS AN ERROR

"Hi Sam, thanks for checking in! Your estimate is ready and we'll email it to you by 3 p.m. tomorrow. We appreciate your patience."

Which statement requires correction?

A defensible reply should say the team is reviewing the details, avoid an invented delivery date and offer an appropriate follow-up without making unsupported promises.

Actions aren't the same as decisions.

This is the error beginners must learn to catch: an AI summary can sound confident while quietly changing the source's meaning.

FICTIONAL MEETING NOTES
• Maya will update the service FAQ by Friday.
• The supply inventory must be reviewed; no owner or deadline has been assigned.
• The team decided not to publish the pricing guide until Legal approves it.

Which extraction is faithful to the notes?

Instructor standard: Extract explicit facts. Separate actions, decisions and restrictions. If the source is silent, report "Not specified"—don't guess.

A workflow you can actually use.

Turn this short lesson into a seven-day experiment. No inflated ROI numbers. Keep the task simple, track the minutes, and stop if the output is unreliable.

YOUR SEVEN-DAY TEST
1. Choose one repetitive, low-risk task.
2. Measure manual completion time on three examples.
3. Repeat with AI plus human verification.
4. Record factual errors and necessary corrections.
5. Compare medians and determine whether to keep the workflow.
